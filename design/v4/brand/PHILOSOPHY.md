# Teal & Tungsten: MOVA v4 design philosophy

Cinema has graded its night scenes the same way for decades. Shadows go teal, skin and practical lights go tungsten orange. The two hues sit opposite each other on the wheel, so a frame graded this way reads as *made*, not merely captured. MOVA turns that grade into its whole visual system, because the product's promise is the same one: help you make the shot you saw.

**Two hues, two jobs.** Orange is the hand. Every action and every AI suggestion is tungsten orange, so the next step is always the warmest thing on screen. Teal is the eye. Selections, scores, progress and anything MOVA has measured are teal. Amber is used only for coaching and rose only for the fourth shot. Nothing else gets a hue, so the footage stays the most colorful thing in the app.

**Surfaces are night, not black.** Backgrounds are teal-black ink (`#071416`), never neutral grey. Cream text (`#FBF3E8`) replaces pure white, like light on paper rather than light from a screen.

**Type has a voice.** Bricolage Grotesque for display: tight, optical-sized, a little eccentric. Instrument Sans for reading. JetBrains Mono for timecode, durations and scores, the numbers a filmmaker actually reads.

**Icons carry color.** Phosphor duotone, tinted by meaning: teal for data, orange for action, amber for beat. Never generic grey outlines.

**Motion is physical.** Springs, not easings. Shared elements morph between screens. Pow's film-exposure flash marks a capture. Vortex particles mark arrival. Each effect plays once and only at a moment that earns it.

**Texture is grain.** A fixed overlay of film grain at 7%. It never scrolls and never sits on interactive content.
